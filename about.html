Professor of English at TCU, working where writing meets code.

Background

I am a professor in the English department at Texas Christian University, where I have spent more than thirty years teaching writing and, for a good stretch of that, teaching people who did not think of themselves as technical how to build things on the web.

My own work sits between literary form and digital media. I publish poetry, I make experimental digital literature, and I play music with The Deep Sleepers. Those look like three separate practices from the outside. From the inside they are the same problem: how do you give a shape to something so a reader can find their way through it?

That is also the question this course asks, which is why I keep teaching it.

Interests

I am interested in the point where a technical decision becomes a decision about a reader. Semantic markup is the clearest example I know. Choosing a heading over a bold paragraph changes nothing on screen and changes everything for someone listening to the page instead of looking at it.

I am also interested in how AI tools belong in a writing classroom. My working position is that they should sharpen judgment rather than substitute for it, and that the way to find out is to use them in the open, with students, and talk honestly about what they cost.

What I Want to Learn

This term I am building this portfolio in step with my students, doing each lab in the order they do it. I have taught this material for years, but building it live is a different exercise. It surfaces the places where an instruction is clear to the person who wrote it and ambiguous to everyone else.

What I most want out of it is a better sense of where the friction actually is, so the next version of the course spends its effort there.
